# Five whys: why do funders lose deals at intake?

**Observed symptom (from Heron's public positioning and the role description):** slow underwriting teams lose deals to faster funders.

1. **Why are they slow?** Each submission waits in a shared inbox, then takes an underwriter a long block of time before any judgment is applied.
2. **Why does it take so long before judgment?** The first part of the job is clerical: open every attachment, work out what it is, copy numbers into a spreadsheet or CRM, recount NSFs, re-add balances, compare names and EINs across documents.
3. **Why is that clerical work still done by people?** The inputs are inconsistent (different banks, scans, photos, missing pages), so teams do not trust a tool to do it unless they can check its work quickly.
4. **Why can't they check the tool's work quickly?** Most automation returns numbers without showing where they came from or which rule produced a flag. Verifying means redoing the work.
5. **Why does that matter so much?** Because the underwriter is accountable for the decision. Without evidence and a record, the safe choice is to re-scrub by hand, and the time saving disappears.

**Root cause (hypothesis):** the bottleneck is not only extraction accuracy; it is *verifiable* extraction. Speed comes from making the machine's work cheap to trust: evidence per number, rule per flag, a person on every judgment call, and a record of all of it.

**What this concept tests:** if every number carries an evidence chip and every flag shows its rule, can an underwriter go from open to decision-ready in minutes, with no re-scrubbing?

All of the above is reasoning from public information, to be validated with underwriters. None of it is a measured finding about Heron or its customers.
